About Me

I am a Mechanical Engineer and Ph.D. researcher at the University of New Hampshire specializing in computational mechanics and computational materials science.

My research develops predictive computational methods that connect material microstructure with macroscopic mechanical behavior. My work integrates finite element analysis, crystal plasticity, image-based modeling, computational homogenization, density functional theory, molecular dynamics, and machine learning.

My research spans structural metals, porous materials, biomaterials, multifunctional materials, and energy-storage systems.

Research Architecture

From Atoms to Engineering Response

My research connects atomistic-scale mechanisms, microstructural features, crystal deformation, continuum mechanics, and effective engineering behavior.

01 · ATOMIC

Atomistic Modeling

Density functional theory and molecular dynamics for interfaces, nanostructures, and energy materials.

02 · MICROSTRUCTURE

Image-Based Modeling

μCT, EBSD, segmentation, microstructure reconstruction, and representative volume elements.

03 · CRYSTAL

Crystal Plasticity

CPFEM simulation of anisotropy, slip activity, grain-scale deformation, and localized plasticity.

04 · CONTINUUM

Finite Element Modeling

Constitutive modeling and nonlinear finite element analysis of advanced engineering materials.

05 · MACRO

Homogenization

Prediction of effective mechanical properties from heterogeneous and porous material RVEs.

Crystal Plasticity & Computational Mechanics

Developed and validated CPFEM models for metallic materials to investigate deformation mechanisms, anisotropic plasticity, slip-system activity, grain-boundary strengthening, and GND evolution.

Image-Based Computational Modeling

Developed automated computational workflows converting three-dimensional μCT images into finite-element-ready representative volume elements for mechanical analysis.

Multiscale Computational Modeling

Integrated continuum mechanics, crystal plasticity, finite element simulation, and atomistic models to link microscopic mechanisms with macroscopic response.

Computational Materials Science

Applied DFT, molecular dynamics, and machine learning to advanced energy-storage materials, nanomaterials, composites, and functional systems.
