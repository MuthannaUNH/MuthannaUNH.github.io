About Me

I am a Ph.D. researcher in Mechanical Engineering at the University of New Hampshire with research interests in computational mechanics, computational materials science, crystal plasticity, finite element modeling, and multiscale materials simulation.

My research develops predictive computational frameworks that connect microstructural characteristics with macroscopic mechanical response. I work across multiple modeling scales, integrating finite element analysis, crystal plasticity, image-based modeling, computational homogenization, molecular dynamics, density functional theory, and scientific computing.

My broader interests include advanced structural materials, porous materials, biomaterials, multifunctional materials, and energy-related systems.

Research Architecture

From Atoms to Engineering Response

My research spans multiple modeling scales and computational frameworks, linking atomic interactions and microstructural mechanisms to continuum-scale mechanical behavior.

01 · ATOMIC

Atomistic Modeling

Density functional theory and molecular dynamics for energy materials, interfaces, and nanostructured systems.

02 · MICRO

Microstructure

μCT, EBSD, image processing, segmentation, and microstructure reconstruction.

03 · CRYSTAL

Crystal Plasticity

CPFEM modeling of anisotropy, slip activity, local deformation, and grain-scale behavior.

04 · CONTINUUM

Finite Element Modeling

Computational mechanics and constitutive modeling for nonlinear material response.

05 · MACRO

Homogenization

Effective mechanical properties of heterogeneous and porous materials using RVEs.

Crystal Plasticity & Computational Mechanics

Modeling deformation mechanisms, anisotropy, slip-system activity, and localized plasticity.

Image-Based Finite Element Modeling

Automated workflows converting three-dimensional μCT microstructures into simulation-ready RVEs.

Scientific Computing

Python, MATLAB, Fortran, C/C++, workflow automation, data processing, and computational research pipelines.
