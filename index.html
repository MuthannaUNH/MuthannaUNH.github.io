About Me

I am a Ph.D. researcher in Mechanical Engineering at the University of New Hampshire, with research interests spanning computational mechanics, computational materials science, crystal plasticity, finite element modeling, and multiscale simulation.

My work focuses on developing predictive computational methods that connect material microstructure with macroscopic mechanical behavior. My research combines continuum mechanics, finite element analysis, image-based modeling, crystal plasticity, atomistic simulations, and computational homogenization.

I am particularly interested in computational approaches for advanced structural materials, porous materials, biomaterials, and energy-related materials.

Research

My research combines computational and experimental approaches across multiple length scales to study deformation, microstructure, and effective properties of engineering materials.

Crystal Plasticity & Computational Mechanics

Crystal plasticity finite element modeling of metallic materials to investigate deformation mechanisms, anisotropy, slip activity, grain-scale behavior, and microstructure-property relationships.

Image-Based Finite Element Modeling

Development of computational workflows that transform three-dimensional μCT microstructures into finite element representative volume elements for mechanical simulation.

Computational Homogenization

Prediction of effective mechanical properties of heterogeneous and porous materials directly from their three-dimensional microstructures.

Multiscale Modeling

Integration of atomistic, microscale, and continuum modeling approaches to understand material behavior across length scales.

Atomistic Modeling

Application of density functional theory and molecular dynamics simulations to investigate advanced functional and energy-storage materials.

Scientific Computing

Development of Python, MATLAB, Fortran, and C/C++ computational workflows for simulation automation, data processing, and materials modeling.
